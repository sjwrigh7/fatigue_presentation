Griddy Gibbs Calibration

An Efficient statistical inference approach for model calibration using griddy gibbs sampling

Motivation

The primary drawbacks of Bayesian model calibration
  • Computational expense
    • Surrogate model training
    • Surrogate model evaluation during MCMC
  • Identifiability problems caused by the discrepancy term
  • Sensitivity of the Metropolis-Hastings algorithm to step size

Motivation

Computational expense is due to the continuous nature of the computer model and MCMC algorithm
  • Given $\boldsymbol{\theta} \in \boldsymbol{\Theta} \subset \mathbb{R}^P$ and $\mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{X} \subset \mathbb{R}^Q$
  • Computer model is expressed $g: \boldsymbol{\Theta} \times \mathcal{X} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$
Expensive MCMC to draw finite samples of the posterior distribution

Motivation

If the domain and codomain of $g$ were finite...
  • Precompute to draw finite samples of the posterior distribution much more efficiently

Griddy Gibbs sampling

Given data model $f(\mathbf{y}|\theta)$ and prior $\pi(\theta)$
  • Construct grid of $\theta$: $\theta_1,\theta_2,\ldots,\theta_J$
  • For each $j=1,\ldots,J$, compute $q_j = f(\mathbf{y}|\theta_j)\pi(\theta_j)$
  • For each $j=1,\ldots,J$, compute $p_j = \frac{q_j}{\sum_{k=1}^J q_k}$

Discrepancy

Additionally, we can integrate the discrepancy term from the mean to the covariance $$\mathbf{y} \sim \textrm{MVN}(\mathbf{g} + \boldsymbol{\delta},\sigma^2 \mathbf{I}) \rightarrow \mathbf{y} \sim \textrm{MVN}(\mathbf{g} ,\sigma^2 \mathbf{I} + \boldsymbol{\Sigma})$$
  • Preserves the accounting for epistemic error
  • Makes the MCMC much more stable in terms of identifiability

Discrepancy

When in the mean
  • Find $g$ and $\delta$ such that $y \sim \textrm{N}(g+\delta,\sigma^2)$
  • $\delta$ shifts the mean
  • Unidentifiable: $\forall g, \; \exists \delta$ satisfying this

Discrepancy

When in the variance
  • Find $g$ such that $y \sim \textrm{N}(g,\sigma^2 + \delta^*)$
  • $\delta^*$ increases the variance
  • Still potential identifiability issues, but is more stable in estimating $x$

Data Model

Integrating the discrepancy term gives $$\mathbf{y}|\mathbf{X},\boldsymbol{\theta}\sim \textrm{MVN}\{\eta(\mathbf{x}_i,\boldsymbol{\theta}),\sigma^2\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^*\}$$
  • $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^*=\mathbf{I}+{\phi}\mathbf{C}$
  • ${\phi}=\tau^2/\sigma^2$

Likelihood

$$\pi(\mathbf{y}| \boldsymbol{\theta},\sigma^2,\rho,\phi)= \frac{1}{ \sqrt{2\pi \sigma^2|\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^*|}} \exp\left\{-\frac{C(\boldsymbol{\theta},\phi,\rho)}{2\sigma^2}\right\}$$
  • $C(\boldsymbol{\theta},\phi,\rho)=\{\mathbf{y}-\eta(\mathbf{x}_i,\boldsymbol{\theta})\}^T{\boldsymbol{\Sigma}^*}^{-1}\{\mathbf{y}-\eta(\mathbf{x}_i,\boldsymbol{\theta})\}$

Prior Distributions

  • $\pi(\theta_p) \sim \textrm{U}(a_p,b_p)$
  • $\pi(\sigma^2) \sim \textrm{IG}(\alpha_1,\beta_1)$
  • $\pi(\phi) \sim \textrm{IG}(\alpha_2,\beta_2)$
  • $\pi(\rho) \sim \textrm{U}(0,1)$

Joint Posterior

$$ \begin{align*} \pi(\boldsymbol{\theta},\sigma^2,\rho,\phi| \mathbf{y})\propto \\ \pi(\mathbf{y}| \boldsymbol{\theta},\sigma^2,\rho,\phi) \pi(\boldsymbol{\theta})\pi(\sigma^2)\pi(\rho)\pi(\phi) \end{align*} $$

Full Conditionals

$$ \begin{align*} \pi(\boldsymbol{\theta}| \mathbf{y},\sigma^2,\rho,\phi) & \propto \pi(\mathbf{y}| \boldsymbol{\theta},\sigma^2,\rho,\phi) \pi(\boldsymbol{\theta}) \\ \pi(\sigma^2|\mathbf{y},\boldsymbol{\theta},\rho,\phi) & \propto \pi(\mathbf{y}| \boldsymbol{\theta},\sigma^2,\rho,\phi) \pi(\sigma^2)\\ \pi(\rho|\mathbf{y},\boldsymbol{\theta},\sigma^2,\phi) & \propto \pi(\mathbf{y}| \boldsymbol{\theta},\sigma^2,\rho,\phi) \pi(\rho) \\ \pi(\phi| \mathbf{y}, \boldsymbol{\theta},\sigma^2,\rho) &\propto \pi(\mathbf{y}| \boldsymbol{\theta},\sigma^2,\rho,\phi) \pi(\phi) \end{align*} $$

Posterior Sampling

  • Draw $\sigma^{2(s+1)} \sim \textrm{IG}\left(\frac{n}{2}+\alpha_1, \frac{1}{2}C_{j_1^{(s)},j_2^{(s)},j_3^{(s)}} +\beta_1 \right)$
  • Griddy Gibbs sampling for $\boldsymbol{\theta}$, $\phi$, and $\rho$

Posterior Sampling

Draw $\boldsymbol{\theta}^{(s+1)}$ from $\{\boldsymbol{\theta}_1,\ldots,\boldsymbol{\theta}_{J_1}\}$, with probabilities $\{p_{1}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}},\ldots,p_{J_1}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\}$, where $p_{j_1}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}} = q_{j_1}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\{\sum_{k=1}^{J_1} q_{k}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\}^{-1}$ and $$\begin{align*} q_{j_1}^{\boldsymbol{\theta}}=\exp\left\{-\frac{C_{j_1,j_2^{(s)},j_3^{(s)}}}{2\sigma^{2(s+1)}} \right\} \pi(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{j_1}), \end{align*}$$ and set $j_1^{(s+1)}$ to be the index of the sampled value.

Posterior Sampling

Draw $\rho^{(s+1)}$ from $\{\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_{J_2}\}$, with probabilities $\{p_{1}^{\rho},\ldots,p_{J_2}^{\rho}\}$, where $p_{j_2}^{\rho} = q_{j_2}^{\rho}\{\sum_{k=1}^{J_2} q_{k}^{\rho}\}^{-1}$ and $$\begin{align*} q_{j_2}^{\rho}=D_{j_2,j_3^{(s)}}^{-1/2}\exp\left\{-\frac{C_{j_1^{(s+1)},j_2,j_3^{(s)}}}{2\sigma^{2(s+1)}} \right\} \pi(\rho_{j_2}), \end{align*}$$ and set $j_2^{(s+1)}$ to be the index of the sampled value.

Posterior Sampling

Draw $\phi^{2(s+1)}$ from $\{\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_{J_3}\}$, with probabilities $\{p_{1}^{\phi},\ldots,p_{J_3}^{\phi}\}$, where $p_{j_3}^{\phi} = q_{j_3}^{\phi}\{\sum_{k=1}^{J_3} q_{k}^{\phi}\}^{-1}$ and $$\begin{align*} q_{j_3}^{\phi}=D_{j_2^{(s+1)},j_3}^{-1/2} \exp\left\{-\frac{C_{j_1^{(s+1)},j_2^{(s+1)},j_3}}{2\sigma^{2(s+1)}} \right\} \pi(\phi_{j_3}), \end{align*}$$ and set $j_3^{(s+1)}$ to be the index of the sampled value.

The choice of the grid of discrete values of $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ is important.
Consider we can only afford to evaluate 20 grid points

Formulation of Sampling Grid

If we choose the grid of $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ naively, this will result in a poor estimate of the posterior

Formulation of Sampling Grid

If we choose the grid of $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ wisely, we can get a much better estimate

Formulation of Sampling Grid

Turn back to surrogate models!
  1. Solve $g$ over an initial design of $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ values
    • Design $\mathcal{D}$
    • Response $g(\mathcal{D})$
  2. Train a surrogate model on $g$, $\eta$

Formulation of Sampling Grid

  1. Evaluate likelihood using the surrogate model, $L|\eta$
    • Find the maximum likelihood estimate, $\widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{MLE}$
    • Determine the upper and lower asymptotic bounds of the likelihood of $\eta$, $\boldsymbol{\theta}_U = (\theta_{1,U},\theta_{2,U},\ldots,\theta_{P,U})$ and $\boldsymbol{\theta}_L = (\theta_{1,L},\theta_{2,L},\ldots,\theta_{P,L})$

Formulation of Sampling Grid

  1. Generate the grid of $\boldsymbol{\theta}$ in Cartesian product space $[\theta_{1,L},\theta_{1,U}] \times [\theta_{2,L},\theta_{2,U}] \times \cdots \times [\theta_{P,L},\theta_{P,U}]$
    • Achieve a far denser grid in the high posterior density region with fewer computer model runs

Evaluation of Asymptotic Bounds

Evaluation of Asymptotic Bounds

  1. Iterate over $p = 1,\ldots,P$
    1. Set $\boldsymbol{\theta}^0 = \widehat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}_{MLE}$
    2. Evaluate $L^0 = L(\cdot|\eta(\boldsymbol{\theta^0}))$
    3. Set iterator $k = 1$
      1. Set $\theta_p^k = \theta_p^{k-1} + \delta$
      2. Set $\theta_1^k,\ldots,\theta_{p-1}^k,\theta_{p+1}^k,\ldots,\theta_P^k = \theta_1^{k-1},\ldots,\theta_{p-1}^{k-1},\theta_{p+1}^{k-1},\ldots,\theta_P^{k-1}$

Evaluation of Asymptotic Bounds

      1. Evaluate $L^k(\cdot|\eta(\boldsymbol{\theta}^k))$
      2. Calculate difference, $\Delta L = L^{k-1}(\cdot|\eta(\boldsymbol{\theta}^k)) - L^{k}(\cdot|\eta(\boldsymbol{\theta}^k))$
      3. If $\Delta L > 1\textrm{E-}8$, set $k=k+1$ and repeat from 5
      4. If If $\Delta L \leq 1\textrm{E-}8$, set $\theta_{p,U} = \theta_p^k$
